THE OVERVIEW

The Irish Riviera, Still Standing.

The anti-boardwalk shore town — no T-shirt shops, just Queen Anne Victorians, a non-commercial oceanfront promenade, and the cachet of one of the most iconic Jersey Shore addresses.

Spring Lake is the anti-boardwalk shore town. No T-shirt shops, no ice cream stands on the beach — just a 2.2-mile oceanfront promenade, the Essex and Sussex Hotel (now condo residences), Spring Lake itself in the town center, and restored Queen Anne Victorian mansions that make it look like Cape May's wealthier cousin.

Known as the Irish Riviera for the Irish-Catholic families who built out the town in the late 1800s, Spring Lake is Monmouth County's most consistent $2M+ luxury market. Oceanfront Victorians start at $3M and go to $10M+; inland Queen Annes and Craftsmans run $1.5M to $3M. The Manasquan Regional High School district serves here, and Spring Lake has its own K-8.

LIFESTYLE

What You'll Find Here.

Spring Lake Oceanfront Boardwalk

The 2.2-mile non-commercial promenade — no arcades, no food stands, just ocean, dune, and the Victorian mansions behind.

Essex & Sussex Residences

The 1914 beaux-arts oceanfront hotel converted to luxury condominium residences — the defining silhouette of the Spring Lake skyline.

Spring Lake (the lake itself)

The freshwater lake in the town center — swan boats, a gazebo, and the landmark after which the borough is named.

St Catharine's Church

The 1901 Italianate Catholic church — a landmark for the Irish-Catholic families who built Spring Lake and still a summer gathering point.

Third Avenue Village Center

The boutique-and-café main street — small inns, a bookshop, restaurants, and the year-round heart of the borough.

Spring Lake Bath and Tennis Club

The private oceanfront club — the social anchor that many Spring Lake families have been members of for generations.

Victorian Historic District

The concentration of restored Queen Anne, Shingle, and Italianate mansions — one of the most intact Victorian streetscapes on the East Coast.

NJ Transit Spring Lake Station

The North Jersey Coast Line stop — an 85-minute direct ride to Penn Station, walkable from the village center.
